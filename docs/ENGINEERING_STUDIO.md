# Engineering Studio — CAD, thermal, structural, vibration and DC studies

Open `engineering-studio.html` from **Admin → Engineering Studio** or the link in the existing IEM Design Studio. Production access uses the existing admin authentication. The store, IEM tools, circuit editor, acoustic engine and reconstruction service retain their existing behavior.

The full platform is still in progress; [requirement coverage and remaining work](ENGINEERING_PROGRESS.md) track the original scope.

This release implements dimensioned profiles and line/circle constraint sketches with extrusion/revolution, ordered union/subtract/intersect features, edge-selected fillets/chamfers, a parametric block with editable cylindrical through-holes, validated single-solid STEP import, Open CASCADE solid validation and STEP/STL export, a shared material library with scalar property curves, optional local Gmsh tetrahedral volume meshing and CAD thermal FEM, static structural elasticity, undamped eigenfrequency/mode analysis, complex harmonic response, Rust DC electrical conduction, and the uncut-block thermal workflow with temperature/convection boundaries, parameter sweeps in Optimize, mesh-convergence checks in Simulate and measurement import/comparison in Analyze. It does not implement the full multi-phase platform specification.

## Try the complete workflow

1. In **Design**, edit `length`, `width` or `height` and apply. Equations can reference other parameters, such as `width = height * 2`. Invalid dimensions and circular dependencies reject the entire edit.
2. Select **Parametric block** to change which expressions define X/Y/Z or assign a material. Select **Material** to browse, duplicate, edit and assign library materials, including constant properties or unit-aware curves. Included values are illustrative, not grade-specific certified data.
3. In **Simulate**, independently assign **Prescribed temperature** or **Convection to ambient** to the minimum and maximum faces of X, Y or Z. Convection requires an ambient temperature and a positive heat-transfer coefficient in `W/(m^2*K)`; these fields can reference global parameters. The other four faces are insulated. Celsius literals (`80 degC`, `20 °C`) and kelvin expressions are accepted. Optional uniform volumetric heat generation uses `W/m^3`.
4. Select **Volume mesh**, choose a preset or cell counts, apply, and **Generate mesh**. The structured mesh is represented procedurally by its exact bounds and cell counts. The viewport grid displays its boundary faces, not interior cells. Solve also generates the mesh automatically.
5. Click **Solve thermal**, then **Analyze**. Inspect the temperature field, the cross-section-averaged temperature profile, heat balance, and an interpolated probe at any point inside the solved block. Export the profile as CSV or change the display range.
6. Save a named input version. Change `length` from `60 mm` to `120 mm`. The previous solution becomes **outdated**, and Analyze continues to display that solution's original geometry. Solve again to produce a separate result for the new geometry.
7. **Export project** creates a portable `.hcengineering.json` containing the source, up to eight recent solutions, five sweep records, three mesh checks (including their fields), six measurement datasets, three CAD tetrahedral meshes, three CAD thermal FEM solutions and three structural solutions, three modal sets, two harmonic sweeps and three DC electrical solutions with their own meshes, and twenty named input versions. **Open** validates the file before replacing the accepted project. Imported numerical solutions are labeled unverified until solved again.
8. In **Design → Hole features**, add a through-hole. Its center and diameter accept unit-aware parameter equations. Select X, Y or Z as its through axis; center coordinates refer to the other two axes, measured from the block's minimum corner. Apply changes to regenerate downstream geometry. Up to eight holes may intersect. Suppress, remove, undo or restore a named version to change the feature stack.
9. In **Manufacture**, wait for **Validated · Open CASCADE**, then export the final solid as STEP or binary STL. STEP includes exact surfaces and millimetre units. STL coordinates are in millimetres; select millimetres in a slicer because STL itself has no unit metadata. Export the project separately to preserve editable history.
10. **Import STEP** accepts a complete text `.step` or `.stp` file up to **1 MiB** containing one closed solid. Try [the sample mounting flange](engineering/examples/mounting-flange.step). Import replaces the base model only after the kernel validates and tessellates it; Undo restores the prior model. The imported body inspector reports its source file, measured volume and original XYZ bounds. Materials stay assigned from the current project; review density before relying on its mass. Use **Use a new parametric block** to return to block modeling, or restore a named version.
11. For an uncut block, open **Optimize**. Sweep `length` from `30 mm` to `120 mm` with seven samples, minimize mass, and optionally limit mass to `40 g`. Run the sweep, compare the table and objective plot, then **Apply #…** to restore a candidate's complete input snapshot. **Solve thermal → Analyze** computes its field again. Undo restores the design displaced by Apply.
12. In **Simulate → Mesh convergence**, choose starting X/Y/Z cell counts, two to four grids, and absolute change limits. Run the check. **Analyze #…** opens a retained field without another solve; **Use #…** restores that grid's complete study inputs. Start from `6 × 3 × 3` and choose four levels to compare 54, 432, 3,456 and 27,648 volume cells.
13. In **Analyze → Measurements**, choose **Import measurement CSV**. Preview the delimiter, header setting, X/Y columns, quantities and units, then **Add dataset to project**. For position versus temperature, specify the model axis and select up to three saved solutions. Compare the plot and error table; export either SI measurement values or per-point comparisons. Removal and import are undoable.

Sketch-based solids, active holes and imported STEP solids disable the original block thermal study; use **CAD thermal study** for these geometries. Existing boundary references are retained but are not attached to new CAD faces. Analyze still displays a saved solution's original uncut geometry, marked outdated. Suppress or remove every downstream operation on a parametric block to run the restricted thermal study again. The Design viewport's Mesh checkbox displays CAD surface tessellation. **Simulate → Tetrahedral mesh** generates and displays actual volume elements separately.

The default block is 60 × 20 × 10 mm, with conductivity 167 W/(m·K), density 2700 kg/m³, and 80 °C / 20 °C on the X faces. Its volume is 12,000 mm³, mass 32.4 g, exact centre temperature 50 °C, and heat flow 33.4 W. Doubling its length halves the heat flow to 16.7 W.

For a convection example, keep the default dimensions/material, set both faces to convection with ambient temperatures 80 °C / 20 °C, coefficients 100 / 200 W/(m²·K), and zero generation. Heat flow is approximately 0.781287 W; the solid surface temperatures are 40.935673 °C / 39.532164 °C. The ambient temperatures are outside the solid and do not set its displayed extrema.

## General CAD tetrahedral meshing

The optional local Gmsh service meshes the exact final CAD solid, including sketches, holes, ordered Boolean operations, fillets/chamfers and imported STEP. Install/start it using the [local compute setup](../tools/engineering-compute/README.md). The service binds to `127.0.0.1:8766`; it is a local compute adapter, not a deployed public backend. A remotely hosted website needs an appropriately secured compute service and browser connectivity configuration.

1. Open **Simulate → Tetrahedral mesh** and **Check local mesher**.
2. Choose Draft, Normal, Fine, Very Fine or Extreme. These derive maximum size from the CAD diagonal divided by 8, 12, 20, 30 or 40, minimum size at one quarter of maximum, and curvature-circle resolutions of 8, 16, 24, 32 or 40. Or enter unit-aware global-parameter expressions for min/max size and curvature resolution (0–64).
3. **Apply mesh settings** stores a `gmsh-tetra` definition alongside the existing structured thermal mesh. **Generate tetrahedral mesh** also applies valid drafts. Each job exports the exact current STEP snapshot, submits SI sizing and independently measured CAD volume, and retains the source revision and expressions.
4. The local service runs Gmsh 4.15.2 in a separate process. The UI stays editable; **Cancel** terminates computation. A newer project cannot receive an older project's mesh. Results for earlier source inputs are retained and clearly marked outdated.
5. Inspect nodes, tetrahedra, boundary triangles, volume difference and quality. Select X/Y/Z and change **Keep cells below** to reveal actual interior elements. The cutaway removes whole cells by centroid; it is stepped, not a planar cut or a source edit.
6. **Export tetrahedral MSH · metres** exports Gmsh 2.2 ASCII nodes, first-order tetrahedra and boundary triangles grouped by CAD surface tag. Coordinates are **metres**, unlike CAD STEP/STL exports in millimetres.

Up to three completed volume meshes persist in the project, retaining coordinates, connectivity, surface groups, mesher version/algorithm, STEP SHA-256, source snapshot, settings, timestamp and input fingerprint. Named versions retain input settings; the mesh records stay in the project. Imported project meshes are marked unverified. Old meshes remain inspectable/exportable without a running service.

The service imports STEP into SI metres and checks that its exact volume agrees with the browser CAD snapshot within `1e-5` relative difference. It generates first-order Delaunay tetrahedra with a fixed seed, one CPU thread, configured size bounds/curvature and optimization. The browser recomputes positive cell volumes, mean-ratio quality, connected regions and complete outward boundary connectivity. Duplicate/inverted/degenerate cells, missing boundaries, unsupported metadata and mesh/CAD volume difference above 5% are rejected. Quality is `12 (3 V)^(2/3) / sum(edge length squared)`: an equilateral tetrahedron scores 1. Cells below 0.1 are flagged. Validity and quality checks do not certify physical solution accuracy or mesh convergence.

Limits: one solid, 50,000 nodes, 100,000 tetrahedra, 90 seconds per job, 8 MiB STEP input, 32 MiB mesh response. An estimated oversized request fails before generation; actual node/cell caps are also checked afterward. Surface tags identify faces within that meshing snapshot, not stable physics references across regeneration. Local/adaptive refinement, boundary layers, higher-order elements and physics-aware sizing remain unavailable. **CAD thermal study**, **Static structural** and **Eigenfrequency study** consume these tetrahedral meshes; the original block study retains its separate structured grid.

## CAD thermal finite-element study

1. Generate a tetrahedral mesh, then open **Simulate → CAD thermal study → Create CAD thermal study**. A compatible locally computed mesh is required; imported meshes must be regenerated before a new solve. Geometry and sizing determine mesh reuse, so material/condition changes do not force remeshing.
2. Click a visible CAD surface or use the surface selector. Apply a fixed temperature or convection coefficient/ambient temperature. All unassigned surfaces are insulated. Set a uniform volume heat-generation term, which can reference global parameters. At least one temperature or positive convection condition is required. Different fixed temperatures cannot meet at shared nodes.
3. **Solve CAD thermal study** runs scikit-fem 12.0.2 with linear tetrahedra, constant isotropic conductivity, and SciPy 1.18.1 conjugate gradients in a separate local process. Editing stays available. Cancellation and failures preserve earlier fields.
4. Open **CAD thermal results** for temperature, heat-flux magnitude or temperature-gradient magnitude. Temperature is interpolated from nodal values; flux/gradient are constant per element. Automatic/custom legend ranges and X/Y/Z whole-cell cutaways are view settings. Min/max describe the full saved field, even while cut away.
5. Inspect volume-mean temperature, iteration count, equation backward error, internal generation, combined fixed-temperature reaction and integrated convection loss. Outward heat is positive. Export nodal CSV or a legacy ASCII VTK unstructured grid with nodal temperature and cell-vector flux/gradient. Coordinates and fields are SI; temperature CSV/VTK uses kelvin.

The weak form assembles `∫ k ∇T·∇v dV + ∫ h T v dA = ∫ Q v dV + ∫ h T∞ v dA`, with prescribed nodal temperatures eliminated. The solver applies Jacobi-preconditioned CG to temperature increments relative to the mean boundary temperature (`rtol=1e-10`, `atol=1e-12`, at most 5,000 iterations). P1 gradients produce `q = −k ∇T`. The reported equation backward error is the free-node residual norm divided by `norm(|A| |T| + |b|)`; it is distinct from discretization error. CG failure, invalid temperatures, excessive residual or heat-balance failure rejects the result. No analytical formula substitutes for a computed field.

CAD conditions contain stable reference IDs, surface type, exact CAD area/center descriptors, feature support and the resolved CAD geometry identity. Surface tags are derived per mesh. Retessellation can preserve assignments even if tags change. **Geometry changes require explicit reselection**; the implementation does not silently trust similar descriptors after an edit. Missing/ambiguous matches reject solving. Automatic topology remapping through shape changes remains unfinished.

Each saved solution includes its immutable source, complete actual mesh, solver versions/settings, temperatures, gradients, flux and diagnostics. The browser checks topology, fixed values, gradient consistency, Fourier's law, volume mean and energy accounting before retention. Imported results are labeled unverified. Upstream edits mark saved results outdated; they remain viewable on their own geometry. This study does not yet participate in block-only sweeps, measurements, probes or automated mesh-convergence checks. Radiation, transient heat transfer, contact, nonlinear/tensor conductivity and multiple regions remain unavailable.

The numerical library interfaces are documented by [scikit-fem](https://scikit-fem.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api.html) and [SciPy CG](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.sparse.linalg.cg.html). See the [CAD thermal verification report](reports/2026-10-12-engineering-fem/README.md).

## Static structural finite-element study

1. Generate a verified tetrahedral mesh, then choose **Simulate → Static structural → Create static structural study**. The existing mesh can be reused after material or load changes. Geometry changes require explicit surface reselection and a compatible mesh.
2. In **Material**, assign constant Young’s modulus (`Pa`) and Poisson ratio (dimensionless). Optional constant yield strength enables a yield/von-Mises comparison. Nonzero gravity requires constant density; a density curve’s reference mass is insufficient. The illustrative built-in materials do not invent missing elastic properties.
3. Click a visible CAD surface or use **Structural CAD surface**. Apply fixed XYZ, prescribed global displacement components (each axis can remain free), a total XYZ surface force, or pressure. A surface has one condition. Total force is distributed uniformly over its actual meshed area; positive pressure acts inward, negative pressure outward. Unassigned surfaces are traction-free. Set gravity in global XYZ using `m/s^2`.
4. **Apply structural conditions**, then **Solve static structural**. Conflicting displacements at shared nodes and insufficient restraints of the six rigid-body motions fail explicitly. Jobs are isolated and cancellable; saved fields survive failure/cancellation and source edits.
5. **Structural results** shows displacement magnitude (µm), von Mises and maximum/minimum principal stress (MPa), or the strain tensor norm. Strain and stress are constant per tetrahedron. Set legend limits, choose X/Y/Z whole-cell cutaways, and apply a deformation display scale. Scale 0 is undeformed, 1 is actual displacement; larger scales only exaggerate the display. Neither the CAD nor saved fields change. Scale is limited to 1,000,000 and ten model diagonals of displayed deflection.
6. Inspect strain energy, solver convergence, applied/support forces and moments. Moments use the mean mesh-node position as their origin. Export nodal displacement/reaction CSV or VTK containing the original mesh, displacement/reaction vectors, von Mises stress and full stress/strain tensors in SI units. Tensor shear strains are half engineering shear strain.

The native Rust implementation uses vector-P1 tetrahedral isotropic elasticity, with `ε = (∇u + ∇uᵀ)/2` and `σ = 2με + λ tr(ε)I`. The Python scikit-fem implementation remains an independent regression reference. Prescribed DOFs are eliminated; Jacobi-preconditioned CG uses `rtol=1e-10`, `atol=1e-12`, at most 10,000 iterations and the shared 90-second wall-time cap. The free-equation backward error must be at most `1e-7`; global force/moment balance is also checked. The browser independently reconstructs strain, Hooke-law stress, von Mises stress, principal invariants, strain energy, prescribed values, internal nodal forces, free-node equilibrium and reactions before retaining a result. Solver identity is shown with the retained result; older Python results preserve their original metadata.

At most three structural results retain their complete mesh, immutable source, software/settings and diagnostics. Imported records are unverified. Small-strain, constant isotropic elasticity on one connected solid is the supported model. Young’s modulus is bounded to 1 kPa–10 TPa; Poisson ratio to −0.99–0.49. Near-incompressible linear tetrahedra can lock; coarse tetrahedra can substantially underpredict bending deflection. Mesh refinement is necessary to assess accuracy. The UI flags near-incompressibility, principal strains above 1% and deflection above 5% of the model diagonal; these are diagnostic thresholds, not validity certification.

Contact, plasticity, springs/bearings, dedicated torque loads, thermal expansion, multiple regions, large deformation, dynamics, structural sweeps and automated structural convergence remain unavailable. Yield/von Mises is only a comparison with the supplied elastic material strength; it is not a general safety factor or design certification. [Structural verification evidence](reports/2026-10-12-engineering-structural/README.md) includes exact patch tests and a bending refinement benchmark. The [Rust port report](reports/2026-10-15-engineering-rust-structural/README.md) records native/WASM parity, independent Python comparisons and portable-project validation.

## Eigenfrequencies and natural modes

1. Generate a verified CAD tetrahedral mesh, then open **Simulate → Eigenfrequency study → Create eigenfrequency study**. Assign constant Young’s modulus, Poisson ratio and density in the shared material library. Yield strength and damping do not affect this undamped eigenproblem.
2. Choose CAD surfaces and apply **Fixed XYZ** or **Zero displacement components** with independent free/fixed global axes. Modal restraints are separate from the static study. No static force, gravity, prestress or nonzero prescribed displacement is applied. Leaving every surface free is supported.
3. Request 1–12 modes and **Apply modal restraints → Solve eigenfrequencies**. Requested modes include any unconstrained rigid motions. A free connected solid has six zero-frequency rigid modes; partial supports can retain fewer. Request more than six to inspect elastic free-body modes.
4. **Analyze → Natural modes** lists frequencies and effective translational masses. Select a mode from the properties panel or the frequency table. The field is the dimensionless shape magnitude, normalized so the largest node vector has magnitude 1. Eigenvector sign is canonicalized; repeated/closely spaced eigenspaces can rotate between meshes.
5. Set a peak display amplitude of 0–25% of the model diagonal. **Play mode animation** uses an illustrative 0.1–3 cycles/second, capped at 20 display updates/second. Playback does not use the physical natural frequency. Pause or set the signed phase manually; X/Y/Z whole-cell cutaways remain available. Fixed full-cycle bounds prevent animation-driven camera rescaling. Navigation, solving, mode changes and hiding the page stop playback. Rigid modes can be inspected at a signed phase but are not played as elastic oscillations.
6. Export the frequency/effective-mass table as CSV, or the selected dimensionless nodal shape and original SI mesh as VTK. Up to three mode sets retain immutable source, mesh, eigenfrequencies/shapes, mass/stiffness, participation, solver settings and residuals. Source edits mark older results outdated; imported results are unverified. Named versions retain study inputs. Reload and inspection of saved modes do not require the local service.

The solver uses the shared vector-P1 isotropic stiffness `K` and a consistent mass matrix `M`, solving `Kφ = λMφ`, `f = √λ/(2π)`, through pinned SciPy `eigsh`. A deterministic starting vector and negative shift `−10⁻⁶ E/(ρL²)` (L = mesh bounding diagonal) avoid singular factorization for free-body modes without adding stiffness to the physical problem. A geometric restraint-rank check identifies rigid-mode count. Returned modes pass residual, Rayleigh quotient and mass-orthogonality checks. Partial ARPACK convergence is rejected rather than silently returning fewer modes.

For the peak-normalized shape, modal mass is `φᵀMφ`, modal stiffness is `φᵀKφ`, global-axis participation is `(φᵀMr)/modalMass`, and effective mass is participation squared times modal mass. The influence vector r is uniform translation on the full mesh. Reported percentages use the whole mesh mass. Truncation means a finite set need not capture all effective mass; participation is not a displacement response amplitude. The browser independently integrates element stiffness/mass, checks free-DOF eigen-equations, normalization/restraints, frequencies, rigid-mode count, participation and pairwise mass orthogonality before retention.

Limits: one connected isotropic solid, 10,000 nodes, 40,000 tetrahedra, 1–12 modes, 90 seconds and 32 MiB returned output. Sparse shift-invert factorization has no OS memory quota. Linear tetrahedra can overpredict bending frequencies, and near-incompressible solids can lock; assess mesh convergence. This implements undamped, unprestressed eigenfrequencies and shapes. Prestress/geometric stiffness, contact and structural transient response remain unfinished. Harmonic response, Rayleigh damping and conditional driving-point impedance are described below. [Modal verification](reports/2026-10-12-engineering-modal/README.md) includes a dense independent mass-matrix comparison, stiffness/density scaling, free-body mass completeness and an axial continuum refinement benchmark.

## Material library and property curves

**Design → Material** opens the project library (1–32 entries). The library picker changes which entry you edit; **Assign to body** changes the single body's material. **Duplicate material** creates an independent copy with a new ID. Unassigned entries can be removed; Undo/Redo restores library edits and assignments. Density and thermal conductivity remain required for compatibility with this release. Optional properties are absent until explicitly supplied; included constants are illustrative, not a certified material database.

Use **Property to add → Add material property**, enter a value or curve, then **Apply changes**. The supported scalar properties and SI units are:

| Domain | Properties and SI units |
| --- | --- |
| General | Density `kg/m^3` |
| Mechanical | Young's modulus, yield strength and ultimate tensile strength `Pa`; isotropic Poisson ratio and damping ratio dimensionless |
| Thermal | Conductivity `W/(m*K)`; specific heat `J/(kg*K)`; linear expansion `1/K` |
| Electrical | Conductivity `S/m`; resistivity `ohm*m`; absolute permittivity `F/m` |
| Magnetic | Absolute permeability `H/m`; coercivity `A/m`; remanence `T`; single-valued B–H table `T` versus `A/m` |
| Acoustic / fluid | Sound speed `m/s`; dynamic viscosity `Pa*s`; amplitude attenuation `Np/m` |

All except B–H accept a constant expression or a **piecewise-linear table** of 2–128 points versus temperature, frequency, absolute pressure or engineering strain. B–H requires a table versus magnetic field H with increasing H and nondecreasing B; hysteresis is unsupported. Each property has one input axis. This is a property-data model, not a plasticity, damage, relaxation or other constitutive solver.

Enter one row per line as `input ; property value`, for example `20 degC ; 70 GPa`. Both columns accept global parameter expressions and compatible units. Input coordinates must increase strictly. Interpolation uses the two surrounding SI values. **Reject extrapolation** is the default; **Use nearest endpoint** explicitly clamps outside the table range. Negative absolute temperature/frequency/pressure, incompatible units, invalid property bounds and duplicate coordinates fail without replacing accepted data. The graph and **Evaluate property** show actual interpolation; changing the evaluation probe does not edit the source or invalidate results.

Variable density requires an explicit matching **Reference condition for mass**. Density is evaluated there and multiplied by the current geometric volume; the UI labels this **Reference mass**. These conditions do not set physics boundaries, deform geometry or account for thermal expansion. Mass sweep objectives and limits also use each sample's explicit reference condition; CSV records the axis, SI coordinate and endpoint-clamping status beside mass. Density does not affect the steady-state temperature solution.

Both thermal solvers accept **constant, isotropic conductivity only**. A conductivity curve is retained as material data but disables both thermal solves and block sweeps/convergence checks; tetrahedral mesh generation remains available. Static structural analysis consumes constant Young’s modulus and Poisson ratio, optional constant yield strength, and constant density when gravity is nonzero. Other electrical/magnetic/acoustic properties are retained for future consumers. Tensor, complex-valued, multi-variable or hysteretic properties, multiple body/region assignments and certified datasets remain unavailable.

**Grade, source & notes** records the material condition and reference supplied by the user. **Export material JSON** writes `hc-material-1`, a portable snapshot with resolved SI values and source notes. **Import material JSON** validates a snapshot (at most 512 KiB), assigns a fresh ID and adds it to the library without changing body assignment. Project JSON retains the original parameter expressions and exact tables; a portable material snapshot intentionally has no external parameter dependencies.

Additional unit aliases include `S`, `kS`, `MS`, `T`, `mT`, `Wb`, `MHz`, `bar`, `psi`, `uF`/`µF`, `nF`, `pF`, `percent` and `Np`. Pre-existing parameter names that match these newly introduced aliases retain precedence; rename such a parameter to use that alias as a unit, or use its base-SI expression. Previously reserved unit names remain reserved. SI dimensional conventions follow [NIST's SI unit reference](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/metric-si/si-units).

Current/outdated status compares canonical source snapshots, not only revision numbers.

Opening a grid in Analyze also retains that field among the eight recent solutions, without duplicating a previously retained field ID. Unverified imported fields remain unverified in both places.
- Up to six measurement datasets retain the original UTF-8 file text, mapping, units, model position axis, name, stable ID and import timestamp. Autosave, recovery and project export include them. Import preview is a local draft until added; leaving the page warns about a pending import. Solver workers never receive measurement files. Dataset edits advance the project revision without changing simulation-input fingerprints.
- Original STEP text is embedded in the source model, so autosave, duplicate, named versions and portable project files need no external CAD file. Project JSON files are limited to 64 MiB. Repeated versions containing large STEP data increase file size; duplicate current inputs to export a smaller project. STEP headers may contain author and originating-system metadata; exporting the project retains those original headers.

## Numerical method and assumptions

`thermal.mjs` contains a small deterministic finite-volume implementation for this restricted problem. The CAD runtime does not extend its physics domain. Gmsh and scikit-fem provide the separate CAD volume-mesh/FEM workflow described above.

The equation is `−∇·(k ∇T) = q` in a homogeneous orthogonal solid. Conductivity is positive, constant and isotropic; heat generation is uniform. Each of two opposite faces has either a prescribed constant temperature or convection to a constant ambient temperature with uniform coefficient. The remaining faces have zero normal heat flux. There is no radiation, material interface, or temperature dependence. Convection uses `q″out = h_c (Tsurface − Tambient)`; the coefficient is a user-specified input, not computed from airflow.

The actual numerical solve uses a three-dimensional cell-centred control-volume grid with a conservative seven-point operator. With cell spacing `Δx`, internal-face conductance is `k A / Δx`; a prescribed-temperature boundary has half-cell distance and conductance `2 k A / Δx`. Convection adds a film resistance, giving `G = A / (Δx/(2k) + 1/h_c)`. Heat rate is `G (Tcell − Tambient)`, and the surface temperature is recovered from the same two resistances. The resulting symmetric positive-definite system is solved by Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients. The operator sums neighbor temperature differences to reduce cancellation. The relative residual target is `1e-10`, with a 6,000-iteration cap and an independently recomputed final residual check. Nonconvergence and invalid absolute temperatures return an error, never fabricated results. Very weak films relative to solid conductivity can be ill-conditioned and fail this residual check; a coarser mesh may help.

Supported boundaries and uniform material make the continuum solution one-dimensional along the selected axis. That symmetry supplies independent analytical benchmarks for the 3D numerical implementation:

- With prescribed temperatures and no generation: `T(x) = T₀ + (T₁ − T₀)x/L` and heat flow `k A (T₀ − T₁)/L`.
- With prescribed temperatures and generation: `T(x) = T₀ + (T₁ − T₀)x/L + q x(L − x)/(2k)`. Mesh refinement checks second-order temperature convergence.
- For mixed or two-film boundaries, let `T₀,T₁` be each prescribed or ambient temperature, and `r₀,r₁` be zero for prescribed temperature or `1/h_c` for convection. The outward minimum-face flux is `j₀ = [T₁ − T₀ + q L (r₁ + L/(2k))] / [r₀ + L/k + r₁]`. Then `T(x) = T₀ + j₀(r₀ + x/k) − q x²/(2k)`. Without generation, the heat flow from minimum to maximum is `A(T₀ − T₁)/(r₀ + L/k + r₁)`.
- Outward boundary heat rates must sum to `q × volume`. Negative outward rate means heat enters the block.

The source-free references are [MIT's one-dimensional conduction notes](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node117.html) and [combined conduction/convection resistance model](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/notes/node123.html). Energy generation is also checked directly through the quadratic solution and conservation, independently of the implementation.

Field display and probes interpolate computed cell values, using the actual solid surface temperatures at the two boundary faces. These equal the prescribed temperatures for fixed faces and are recovered from the film/half-cell resistances for convection. The plotted profile averages each transverse cell layer and both end surfaces. Temperatures are solved in kelvin and displayed in Celsius. Range controls affect colors only. Result extrema include sampled cells and solid surface values, never the ambient air temperatures; an interior continuum maximum may require refinement to resolve.

## Parameter sweeps

Optimize runs 2–21 uniformly spaced samples of one existing global parameter, including both endpoints. Endpoints accept compatible engineering units or expressions evaluated against the original input snapshot. The selected parameter's expression is replaced by a numeric SI value for each sample; all dependent expressions regenerate. Material and thermal parameters can also be swept when the corresponding fields reference them. Sweeping an unused parameter produces identical physical results.

Every valid sample invokes the same three-dimensional thermal solver, tolerance and iteration limit used by **Solve thermal**. Invalid geometry and failed solves produce diagnostic rows without fabricated measurements; subsequent samples continue. Active holes and imported STEP solids remain unsupported. Grid cell counts stay fixed, so physical cell sizes vary with the block dimensions. This is a design comparison at the chosen discretization, not a mesh-convergence study.

Objectives are minimum mass, minimum maximum temperature, or maximum **signed outward heat rate at the maximum face of the selected axis**. Optional upper bounds on mass and temperature filter feasible candidates. Equality is included with a rounding allowance of `1e-12 × max(1, |limit in SI|)`. Temperature extrema include computed cells and solid surfaces; source-free studies with unchanged prescribed temperatures can therefore tie on maximum temperature. Convection coefficients and ambient temperatures can be swept through referenced global parameters. The earliest sample wins exact ties. “Best tested” is the best feasible successful sample so far, not a guaranteed continuous or global optimum. Cancelled runs are explicitly partial. The chart plots only solved samples and does not infer a response between them.

A record stores the original source snapshot and fingerprint, project/revision, timestamp, solver settings, sweep method/version, parameter/range/objective/limits, and ordered sample outcomes. Each successful row also has an exact input fingerprint, SI metrics, residual and iteration count. Applying a sample restores its full geometry, material, physics and mesh snapshot as one undoable edit; it can restore earlier inputs if the current design has changed. Sweep fields are not retained: solve an applied design to inspect a fresh field in Analyze. **Use saved setup** copies settings for a new run against the current source.

**Export sweep CSV** includes SI values, energy balance, convergence, feasibility and run provenance. **Export project** preserves complete source snapshots needed to reconstruct candidates. Project file imports reset sweep metrics to unverified, just as they do saved thermal fields. Adaptive search, multiple simultaneous variables, Pareto analysis, sensitivities and generative optimization are not implemented.

## Mesh-convergence checks

The check holds geometry, materials, boundary conditions, source term and solver settings fixed. Each grid doubles the cell count in **every** direction, halves all three spacings and multiplies the total cell count by eight. Plans contain two to four grids; the entire plan must fit the existing 64-cells-per-axis and 32,768-total-cell limits before a worker starts. The starting grid can differ from the current project mesh. Applying a grid restores its complete snapshot, including any earlier design inputs, as one undoable edit.

Each field is solved independently. A failed grid has a diagnostic row, and no fabricated field or comparison is supplied. The report derives all measurements from retained fields instead of storing editable summary values. Only adjacent successful grids are compared; a failed intermediate level cannot be silently skipped. Current/outdated status compares the original complete source snapshot with the current project.

The two change measures are:

- Maximum absolute difference, in kelvin, between cross-section-averaged temperature profiles. Each profile is linearly interpolated between its cell-layer means and solid surface endpoints. The maximum is evaluated at the union of both profiles' knots, which captures the maximum of their piecewise-linear difference without relying on a fixed probe spacing.
- Maximum absolute change, in watts, across the two signed outward boundary heat rates.

**Within change limits** means both measures satisfy the requested absolute thresholds on the finest pair of a fully completed check. It is not a discretization-error bound, proof of asymptotic convergence, or certification of the physical model. Cancelled, incomplete, failed and imported/unverified checks cannot receive this assessment. Three or more refinement levels are generally useful for assessing convergence behavior; see [NASA Glenn's grid-convergence tutorial](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/spatconv.html). This feature does not implement Richardson extrapolation or a Grid Convergence Index.

For this restricted homogeneous block, the analytical solution listed above supplies a separate benchmark. **Cell error** is the largest absolute temperature difference at every computed cell center, against that closed-form expression. The chart shows these measured errors for the actual solved grids. With zero generation, the exact field is linear, so mesh changes and errors are near the solver's numerical precision; apparent variations at that scale are not meaningful evidence of an order of accuracy. With uniform generation, the independent cell-error benchmark is `|q| h² / (8k)`, plus iterative/roundoff effects, where `h` is the cell spacing along the conduction axis. It decreases by approximately four on uniform refinement. The analytical check applies only to this model's assumptions.

Example: the default block with `1e7 W/m^3` generation and starting grid `6 × 3 × 3` gives maximum cell errors of approximately `0.748503`, `0.187126`, `0.0467814` and `0.0116954 K` across four levels. The final profile change is approximately `0.0467814 K`, within a `0.1 K` threshold; both boundary heat rates together equal `120 W` generated inside the block. Temperatures are still computed numerically, not substituted from the analytical expression.

**Export mesh check CSV** includes grid sizes, extrema, change measures, analytical cell errors, boundary heat rates, energy balance, residuals, iteration counts, thresholds and run provenance in SI units. **Export project** preserves every retained field and its exact inputs. **Reuse mesh check setup** copies the selected settings for a new run against the current design.

## Measurement import and comparison

The import accepts UTF-8 comma-, semicolon- or tab-separated numeric data, up to 256 KiB, 32 columns and 5,000 points per dataset. Quoted fields, escaped quotes, embedded newlines, CRLF/LF and a UTF-8 BOM are handled using the conventions described in [RFC 4180](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4180). Completely blank records are skipped. Every remaining record must have the same column count. A delimiter and header setting are suggested; review them and the two selected columns before committing. The selected columns must contain finite decimal/scientific numbers. Missing values, formulas, unit-bearing cells and decimal commas reject the whole import with a record/column diagnostic. Unselected columns are preserved in the source text.

Quantity/unit choices use the common unit engine. Position/displacement, time, frequency, temperature, pressure, force, voltage, current, impedance magnitude, volume flow and dimensionless data can be plotted. Values convert to SI internally; Celsius includes its offset. Header names never override the selected units. Duplicate X coordinates and original record order are retained; every replicate receives equal weight. Temperature cannot fall below absolute zero; frequency and impedance magnitude cannot be negative. Imported data is not a claim that instrument accuracy or experimental conditions have been verified.

The implemented simulation comparison is **position versus temperature**, on the solution's conduction axis. Position is distance from that axis's minimum block face, with no automatic offset, scaling, registration or fitting. For the restricted block model the transverse field is uniform, so its cell-layer mean profile provides the comparison curve. Up to three saved solutions can be overlaid, each labeled with current/earlier inputs and imported/unverified status. Their original source snapshots determine their length, mesh, boundaries and material; editing the active design does not modify the comparison curves. Other datasets remain viewable/exportable without invented solver results.

The predicted value at each measured position is linearly interpolated between saved cell-layer means and actual solid surface values. Positions strictly outside a solution's interval are excluded and counted; there is no extrapolation. All measured points are plotted, including excluded ones. With error `e = simulated − measured` and `N` in-domain points, the table reports `RMSE = sqrt(sum(e²)/N)`, `MAE = sum(|e|)/N`, `bias = sum(e)/N`, and maximum absolute error, all in kelvin. No overlap gives no metrics, rather than zeros. These are unweighted descriptive differences; there are no uncertainty intervals, pass/fail limits, calibration or experimental validation claims.

**Export measurement SI CSV** includes source record numbers and import metadata. **Export comparison CSV** includes each compatible selected solution, per-point measured/predicted values, residuals, exclusions, solution IDs and solver provenance. Exported text cells are formula-escaped. **Export project** retains the complete source files and saved solution inputs. The values table displays the first 100 points; plots, metrics and CSV use all points. Dataset and overlay selections are session view state; accepted datasets persist. Frequency log axes, complex impedance/phase, multiple simultaneous measurement overlays and automatic calibration remain future work.

## Dimensioned sketch profiles and solids

Open **Design → Sketch & solid**, choose a starting profile, and **Create sketch-based solid**. Creation replaces the current base geometry and downstream features as one undoable edit; save a named version first if you want a lasting alternative. Existing thermal results retain their own original geometry and become outdated.

The sketch is a separate source object with a stable ID. Its extrusion/revolution references that ID. Rectangles use a lower-left U/V corner and width/height; circles use center/radius; ellipses use center/U radius/V radius; polygons use 3–64 ordered point IDs with U/V coordinate expressions. Global parameters can drive every coordinate, dimension, origin, distance and angle. A polygon closes automatically from the last point to the first; do not repeat the first point. Self-intersections, touching non-neighbor edges, degenerate edges, collinear triples and invalid units are rejected before replacing accepted inputs. Both clockwise and counterclockwise simple polygons are accepted.

**Edit 2D profile / View 3D solid** switches the central viewport. Numbered polygon handles can be dragged with 0.5 mm snapping; this explicitly replaces that point's coordinate expressions with millimetre literals. The coordinate fields provide precise and keyboard-accessible edits. Profile changes remain drafts until **Apply changes**. Invalid polygons remain visible with a diagnostic so they can be repaired, while the previously accepted 3D solid remains unchanged.

Planes have explicit right-handed frames: XY uses U=+X, V=+Y, normal=+Z; XZ uses U=+X, V=+Z, normal=−Y; YZ uses U=+Y, V=+Z, normal=+X. The XYZ origin translates the plane. Extrusion follows its normal; a negative distance reverses it. Revolution uses the sketch V axis through that origin, with a positive right-hand angle from 0.01–360 degrees. Use `270 deg` or a compatible scalar/radian expression. Revolved profiles must stay on the positive U side; circles and ellipses need at least 1 µm separation from the axis to avoid pinched topology.

The local Open CASCADE worker constructs the exact planar wire/curves and solid, validates topology and a single positive-volume body, and reports its measured volume, bounding dimensions and mass using the assigned material density. STEP exports exact geometry; STL exports its tessellation. Source history, named versions, autosave, recovery, duplicate and project JSON retain the sketch and operation expressions. Importing STEP replaces the whole feature stack and removes its active sketches; Undo/named versions retain the prior design.

Example: the default L profile has area 700 mm²; a 10 mm extrusion has volume 7,000 mm³ and mass 18.9 g at 2700 kg/m³. A circle centered at U=20 mm, V=15 mm with radius 10 mm, revolved 270° about V, has volume `0.75 × 2π × 20 × π × 10² = 29,608.8132 mm³`.

Rectangle, circle, ellipse and polygon coordinate profiles retain their explicit dimensions. Choose the constrained-profile type below for independently referenced points/lines/circles and geometric constraints. Each sketch still has one output profile; features can share sketches as described below. Multiple loops, arcs, slots, splines, projection and trim/offset/pattern tools remain to be implemented. Through-holes currently require the original block feature. Sketch-derived solids do not bypass the thermal solver's uncut-block restriction, including rectangular extrusions. [Replicad's planes and sketches documentation](https://github.com/sgenoud/replicad/blob/main/packages/replicad-docs/docs/tutorial-overview/planes-and-sketches.md) describes the adapter's plane-to-solid workflow; the checked-in 1.1.0 runtime remains pinned.

## Constrained line and circle sketches

Choose **Design → Sketch & solid → Constrained rectangle → Create sketch-based solid**, or select **Constrained lines & circles** as an existing feature's profile type. A new constrained profile starts with four directed lines, four points, a fixed origin point, horizontal/vertical constraints and two line lengths. Its 40 × 25 mm rectangle has zero remaining degrees of freedom. All previous coordinate-profile types remain available.

**Points & fixed coordinates** edits initial U/V expressions and the explicit fixed flag. Movable point coordinates are solver seeds, not driving dimensions. **Lines & circles** defines lines by start/end point references and circles by a center point and seed radius. Add and remove entities using their stable IDs. Unused entities are construction geometry and still participate in the solve. Removing a referenced object leaves an explicit validation error until its consumers are repaired or removed.

**Profile entity order** defines the one loop used by CAD: one circle ID, or at least three directed lines in head-to-tail order. Endpoints can use separate point IDs joined by coincident constraints. Construction circles/lines do not automatically create holes. Use a downstream subtract tool for a cut. The source supports at most 64 points, 64 entities and 128 constraints per sketch.

The **Constraint** picker selects a driving rule. Add/remove/suppress rules and set typed references. Supported rules are horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular, coincident points, concentric circles, equal line lengths, equal radii, line/circle tangency, circle/circle tangency, symmetry about a line, point distance, line length, circle radius/diameter, directed line angle and point U/V position. **Fix P…** fixes both point coordinates. Fixed line endpoints fix a line; a fixed center plus radius/diameter fixes a circle. Dimensions use engineering units and global parameter expressions. Angles run counterclockwise from the first directed line to the second; circle tangency follows the local internal/external solution nearest the seeds.

The 2D viewport solves draft changes in a dedicated worker after a short debounce. It displays **Under-constrained** with the remaining degrees of freedom, **Fully constrained** at zero DOF, or **Over-constrained** with conflicting/redundant constraint IDs. Nonconvergence is reported as a solve failure. The **Solved coordinates & radii / mm** disclosure shows the numerical solution without overwriting seed expressions. CAD inspection also reports the applied sketches' statuses while viewing the 3D solid. Constraint selection and disclosure state are view state, not geometry changes.

Apply stores the graph and regenerates every dependent feature using the same pinned PlaneGCS engine in the CAD worker. Parameter changes solve again from the preserved seeds. A fully constrained profile is not automatically a valid solid: open, intersecting, collapsed or out-of-bounds loops are rejected separately. Invalid applied constraints clear the current CAD preview and disable STEP/STL export; repair, suppress a constraint or Undo to recover. Under-constrained profiles may generate solids, but their free geometry depends on the seeds and may change when inputs change. No global solution/uniqueness claim is made for nonlinear sketches.

The runtime is the pinned `@salusoft89/planegcs` 1.3.0 WebAssembly port of FreeCAD PlaneGCS, using DogLeg with 200 iterations and a `1e-10` solver threshold in the millimetre system. Application-level postchecks independently verify every active constraint (absolute tolerance `1e-8 m` for length residuals and `1e-7` for angular residuals), fixed coordinates, finite bounds and noncollapsed entities. Solves and previews are cancellable by replacement and bounded by existing worker timeouts. [Pinned source and notices](engineering/vendor/README.md) document the separate replaceable assets.

Project JSON, history, versions and recovery retain the graph, seed expressions, driving dimensions and stable references. Solved coordinates are derived, never trusted as imported source data. The original thermal provenance stays unchanged. Arcs, elliptical constraints, splines, multiple loops, snapping/projection and general topology attachments still require implementation; this is not the entire requested sketch environment.

## Ordered solid operations

Open **Design → Solid operations**, select Union, Subtract or Intersect, then **Add solid operation**. The starting sketch uses the current solid bounds: an attached rectangular boss, a circular through cut, or a rectangular clipping tool. Initial coordinates are editable expressions independent of faces; they are not automatic face attachments. An arbitrary imported shape may need its initial tool repositioned to overlap correctly.

Each operation references a separate sketch and constructs an extrusion or revolution as its tool. Union adds that tool, Subtract cuts it, and Intersect retains only the overlap. Edit the Boolean operation, profile, plane, origin, distance or angle and apply. For a top-entry blind pocket on the default 10 mm block, set a circular tool's origin Z to `height` and extrusion distance to `-height/2`. With radius 4 mm, its measured volume is `12000 − π × 4² × 5 = 11748.6726 mm³`.

The **History feature** picker selects an operation. **Move earlier/later** changes execution order; **Suppress/Enable feature** preserves its inputs while excluding/including its geometry; **Remove feature** removes it and only sketches no longer referenced by any feature. All changes support Undo/Redo. Up to 24 downstream operations are supported, including at most eight legacy block through-holes. Those holes span the original block extent and execute at their history position; they do not automatically cut features added later.

Use **Referenced sketch** to reuse another feature's profile. The editor lists all consumers and warns that profile or placement edits affect them together. **Make sketch independent** clones that profile with a new stable reference before separate edits. Suppressed features keep their sketch references. Unreferenced sketch objects are rejected on import rather than silently discarded.

The base can be a block, an extruded/revolved sketch or one imported STEP solid. The original STEP remains embedded and unchanged while downstream tools modify its generated shape. Each enabled stage must yield exactly one valid closed solid with positive volume. Empty intersections, full removals or disconnected unions fail at that stage, even if a later feature could restore a solid. The failed feature's name is shown, the obsolete viewport is cleared, and exports remain disabled until the history is repaired or the failing feature is suppressed. Final bounding dimensions, volume and mass come from the kernel.

Project JSON, autosave, recovery and named versions retain the whole editable stack. STEP/STL export its final geometry. Geometry-only cache keys exclude suppressed operations and non-geometric edits. Saved thermal results keep their original block inputs; any active Boolean tool disables the restricted thermal study. Suppressing all downstream operations on a base block re-enables it. Multiple independent bodies, arbitrary component operands, direct face editing, constraints on additional curve types and general topology remapping remain unfinished.

## Fillets and chamfers

Open **Design → Solid operations → New fillet** or **New chamfer**. Select edges by clicking their visible segments in the input viewport or by using the edge checkboxes. The list identifies curve type, bounding-box center and length in millimetres. Amber edges show the current selection. Viewport picking rejects edges hidden behind the solid; the list also allows choosing hidden edges. Surface-mesh display is temporarily disabled while selecting edges.

Enter a unit-aware **Fillet radius** or **Chamfer distance** and create the feature. Fillets use a constant radius; chamfers use equal distances on the two adjacent faces. Parameters and equations are accepted. The existing Open CASCADE runtime constructs and validates the result; these are real surface operations, not tessellation decorations. Tangent chains can be propagated by the kernel. Oversized or incompatible selections fail at the responsible feature and block exports until repaired, suppressed or undone.

Select an existing treatment through **History feature** to edit its size. **Edit edge selection** reconstructs the true input immediately before that feature in a separate worker, even if the final model is invalid. The viewport clearly labels this input preview; final solid volume/export still refer to the complete history. **Show finished solid** exits the input view. **Discard edge edits** abandons unapplied changes. Move, suppress, remove, Undo/Redo and named versions apply to treatments in the same ordered stack as sketch tools and holes.

Each selected edge has a stable reference ID and a geometric anchor: curve type, closed state, bounds and curve midpoint, normalized to its input solid bounds. The reference also records the ordered enabled upstream feature IDs/types. Regeneration requires a unique geometric match within `2e-6` normalized coordinates and unchanged upstream support. Kernel edge indices and the temporary E1/E2 preview labels are never saved as identities. Simple translations and suitable dimension changes preserve references; edits that alter the normalized local geometry can require reselection, including some changes to upstream fillet radii. Reordering/suppressing upstream features also requires review. Missing or ambiguous references fail explicitly; **Start a new edge selection** replaces the affected selection with edges the user chooses. This is limited geometric remapping, not a complete topological naming system or general physics face remapping.

A treatment accepts 1–64 selected edges. Edge selection currently supports solids with at most 512 edges and 150,000 preview line segments; a more complex valid solid can still be viewed/exported without using edge treatments. Variable-radius fillets, unequal-distance/angle chamfers and face-level editing remain unimplemented. Edge-modified geometry remains incompatible with the restricted block thermal solver.

## CAD kernel and geometry lifecycle

The dedicated CAD worker loads a pinned, same-origin OpenCascade.js build (`replicad-opencascadejs` 1.1.0) through the `replicad` 1.1.0 adapter. Its approximately 22 MB WASM file is served with the website, without a runtime CDN. Package versions, build instructions, upstream source links and license notices are in [vendor/README.md](engineering/vendor/README.md). Rebuild assets with `npm ci && npm run build` from `tools/engineering-cad`; the lockfile pins dependencies and the asset manifest records SHA-256 checksums.

Project expressions use SI internally; the adapter converts lengths to millimetres for the kernel and volumes back to cubic metres. It creates an exact base and applies sketch-tool Booleans, original-block cylinder cuts and edge treatments in feature order. Hole circles must remain within the four side walls with at least 1 µm clearance. The kernel checks topology and requires one closed solid with positive volume. It computes the final volume, including intersections between holes, for the displayed mass. Material density is applied to that measured volume.

Dimensions and positions regenerate from source expressions. Persistent IDs identify the base body and editable features; kernel face indices are transient and are never used as persistent physics references. General face remapping, constraints on additional curve types and arbitrary independent-body operands are not implemented in this milestone.

STEP import runs in a separate cancellable worker. The reader explicitly transfers declared source units to millimetres, requires every transferable root to succeed, checks topology, and rejects multiple solids, open surfaces or loose geometry outside the solid. A successful candidate replaces the input model as one undoable edit. Accepted changes or draft edits made during validation prevent that candidate from overwriting the newer work. Opening a project or restoring a named version with STEP data also validates its active imported solid before replacing the current model.

Imported geometry keeps its coordinates; only the camera centers on the measured bounding box. STEP millimetre, metre and inch cases are covered by real-kernel tests. Bounds must span 1 µm–1000 m per axis, have aspect ratio no greater than 10,000, and lie within 10 km of the origin. The imported base does not resize when global parameters change; parameter-driven downstream tools do regenerate. Direct face editing, imported feature history, colors, material metadata, assembly hierarchy and additional CAD formats are not supported. A source assembly containing exactly one solid is treated as that solid, without assembly structure.

CAD generation receives immutable inputs. A newer geometry terminates the old worker and ignores late replies. Export requests must match the generated geometry fingerprint. Failures and a 60-second timeout show an actionable error with retry; the CAD viewport is cleared rather than displaying an obsolete solid as current. Input history remains available for undo/recovery. Material and mesh edits reuse the geometry when its resolved definition is unchanged. Browser surface tessellation is for display; STEP retains exact geometry and STL approximates it with triangles.

## Current limits and next integrations

- One base block, extruded/revolved profile or imported STEP solid, with up to 24 downstream sketch-tool Booleans, fillets/chamfers and legacy block holes (at most eight holes), plus one restricted block steady thermal study and optional CAD thermal, static structural and eigenfrequency studies. Constraint solving currently covers lines/circles only. Direct face editing, independent Boolean operands and multi-solid assemblies remain unavailable.
- A thermal face ID denotes a specific uncut-block-axis side and survives dimensional regeneration. Active downstream operations suspend use of those references. General CAD face remapping is not implemented.
- Block-study thermal boundaries are limited to the two opposite end faces; each can have a prescribed temperature or convection. Coefficients must be between `1e-6` and `1e8 W/(m²·K)`; this input bound does not guarantee numerical convergence. CAD FEM allows arbitrary surface temperature/convection assignments. Airflow, radiation, contact and transient behavior remain unavailable.
- Uniform grids, 2–64 cells per axis, maximum 32,768 volume cells. Mesh checks compare two to four uniformly refined grids. No local refinement, adaptive meshing, curved elements or automatic mesh-convergence certification.
- Additional mechanical studies (harmonic, damped, transient, nonlinear/contact), acoustic, fluid, electromagnetic, circuit and coupled studies remain unavailable here. Existing IEM calculations remain in the existing tools; no claim is made that their data has migrated into this model.
- **Optimize** supports one-variable thermal parameter sweeps with objectives and optional mass/temperature limits. Automated search, sensitivities and multiple simultaneous variables remain unavailable. **Manufacture** provides STEP/STL export and measured volume/mass; printer qualification, wall checks, tolerances, slicing and CAM remain unavailable.
- CAD and the restricted thermal solver run in browser workers; general CAD tetrahedral meshing, thermal FEM, structural FEM and modal FEM use the optional local subprocess service. No public cloud job queue, distributed compute, GPU solver, AI assistant, generative design or scripting API is deployed.

Next work should extend static elasticity to further mechanical studies, improve persistent topology remapping through geometry changes, and add CAD field probes and general mesh-convergence studies. Sketches and additional editable features can extend the same source model while retaining these regression cases and the existing IEM workflow.

## Harmonic response

Create a harmonic study on a compatible verified CAD mesh. Restraints are independent of the static/modal studies. Assign fixed or zero-component displacement surfaces, peak total forces or inward pressures with phases, Rayleigh alpha/beta, and an area-weighted response surface/axis. Apply saves the selected surface condition and sweep settings together.

The Python mechanics adapter solves `(K − ω²M + iω(αM + βK))U = F` with consistent mass and complex sparse direct factorization. Phasors use `exp(+iωt)`. Positive load phase leads the global cosine. Results include complex displacements, cycle-peak displacement/velocity/elastic von Mises fields, mean response amplitude and phase, and average energies/power. Driving-point impedance is available only for a single aligned force on the response surface. Animation evaluates `Re(U) cos θ − Im(U) sin θ`; display scale does not alter the solution. CSV exports sampled responses; VTK retains real/imaginary vector fields.

Limits: one constant isotropic elastic solid, 4,000 nodes / 16,000 tetrahedra / 24 positive frequencies per sweep. Two sweeps are retained. No base excitation, static prestress, nonlinear/contact mechanics or transient response. Frequency grids may miss sharp resonances; refine frequency and spatial resolution. Material damping-ratio properties are not automatically converted into Rayleigh coefficients.

## DC conduction in Rust

Choose **DC conduction**, create a study, and assign constant material electrical conductivity in `S/m`. Apply voltage or total inward current on CAD surfaces; at least one voltage reference is required. Other surfaces are insulated. Voltage terminals must not share mesh nodes. `mV`, `kV`, `uV`/`µV` and `uA`/`µA` are supported alongside `V`, `A` and `mA`; parameters with newly added unit names retain precedence for old-project compatibility.

The native Rust P1 solver returns nodal potential, cell electric field/current density/Joule heat, terminal voltage/current, total heat/input power and conservation errors. Electrical results support retained source/mesh snapshots, staleness, three-result history, cutaways and SI CSV/VTK export. Two voltage terminals also report resistance `ΔV²/P`. One connected isotropic solid, constant conductivity, 50,000 nodes / 100,000 tetrahedra; no AC/circuits, electrostatics, magnetics, contact resistance, temperature feedback or thermal coupling.

The parameter editor is Rust-owned DOM compiled to WASM; most other UI/project/rendering code remains JavaScript. OpenCascade and PlaneGCS remain C++/WASM. The Rust supervisor preserves Gmsh and modal/harmonic Python adapters, with actual capability checks.
