HAMMER CRAFT / ENGINEERING

An IEM is not just a collection of drivers.

Hammer Craft treats the IEM as one acoustic system. Driver behaviour, crossover topology, acoustic damping, sound-tube geometry, chamber volume, venting and nozzle interaction are developed together.

The objective is to move from an intended frequency-response target to a repeatable physical build that can be measured and refined.

SYSTEM DEVELOPMENT

Engineering the response.

01

Drivers.

Dynamic, balanced-armature and other transducers are evaluated as parts of the complete system rather than by their datasheet response alone.

02

Crossover.

Electrical filtering is developed alongside the natural acoustic roll-off and interaction between individual acoustic paths.

03

Damping.

Acoustic resistance, tubing and chamber behaviour are used to control resonances, level and bandwidth.

04

Geometry.

Tube diameter, tube length, nozzle geometry, internal volume and venting can all influence the resulting response.

SOFTWARE-ASSISTED DEVELOPMENT

Target. Measure. Adjust.

Hammer Craft's tuning software is designed around the relationship between the intended response, measured prototypes and the physical parameters used to create them.

As our build database grows, the system can be used to identify which acoustic and electrical changes are most likely to move a design toward its target.

DIGITAL FIT

From photographs to geometry.

Custom-fit orders use a guided image-capture process around each ear. Overlapping photographs are reconstructed into 3D geometry and processed into a clean mesh.

The resulting ear geometry becomes the starting point for an individual shell rather than being treated as a finished printable model.

DEVELOPMENT PROCESS

Digital to physical.

01

Target.

Define the intended acoustic response.

02

Design.

Develop the acoustic architecture, internal geometry and physical shell.

03

Manufacture.

Resin additive manufacturing is used to create complex shell and internal structures.

04

Measure.

The completed build is compared against its intended response using a measurement workflow.
