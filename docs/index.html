HAMMER CRAFT / ENGINEERING

An IEM is not just a collection of drivers.

Hammer Craft treats the IEM as one acoustic system. Driver behaviour, crossover topology, acoustic damping, sound-tube geometry, chamber volume, venting and nozzle interaction are developed together.

The objective is to move from an intended frequency-response target to a physical build that can be measured, compared and refined.

ACOUSTIC ENGINEERING

Engineering the response.

01

Drivers.

Dynamic, balanced-armature and other transducers are evaluated as parts of a complete acoustic system rather than from datasheet response alone.

02

Crossover.

Electrical filtering is developed alongside the natural acoustic roll-off and interaction between individual sound paths.

03

Damping.

Acoustic resistance, tubing and chamber behaviour can be used to control output, bandwidth and resonances.

04

Geometry.

Tube length, diameter, nozzle geometry, internal volume and venting all influence the resulting response.

SOFTWARE + HARDWARE

Target. Measure. Adjust.

The tuning system is designed around the relationship between the intended response, measured prototypes and the physical build parameters that created them.

TARGET RESPONSE

Define.

Start with an intended frequency-response target rather than making random physical adjustments.

PHYSICAL PARAMETERS

Configure.

Driver balance, crossover values, acoustic resistance, tube dimensions, chambers and venting become controllable design variables.

MEASUREMENT

Compare.

Prototype measurements are compared with the target to identify where the physical result differs.

ITERATION

Learn.

Each build adds information about how specific physical changes influence the measured response.

SOFTWARE-ASSISTED DEVELOPMENT

More useful with every measured build.

Hammer Craft's internal software is being developed to connect target response, measured output and physical design parameters.

As the build database grows, the system can help identify which acoustic and electrical changes are most likely to move a design toward its intended response.

DIGITAL FIT

From photographs to geometry.

Custom-fit builds use a guided image-capture workflow around each ear. Overlapping photographs are reconstructed into three-dimensional geometry and processed into a clean anatomical reference.

The reconstructed ear is not treated as the finished shell. It becomes the starting point for CAD development around fit, acoustic architecture and manufacturing constraints.

ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING

Geometry we can actually build.

01

CAD.

Shell shape, internal routing and acoustic structures are developed within the same digital design workflow.

02

Orientation.

Print direction and support placement are chosen around surface quality, drainage and dimensional reliability.

03

Resin.

Fine-layer resin printing makes complex external and internal IEM geometry practical.

04

Finish.

Washing, curing, support removal, surface finishing and assembly complete the physical shell.

MEASUREMENT

A target is only useful if you measure the result.

Prototype and product responses are measured so the physical result can be compared with the intended target.

Measured product response also powers the public tuning matcher, allowing a customer target to be compared against products that actually exist.
